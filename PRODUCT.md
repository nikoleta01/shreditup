# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Slovak surfskate and surf community members, mostly in their 20s and early 30s, with some families who share the love of skating and the outdoors. They attend a 3-day outdoor festival at Camping Sereď. They arrive with the festival poster's visual world in their head and scan a QR code at the entrance. They use the app standing, one-handed, probably in bright sunlight, to check who's on next. Secondary use: the days before the event to plan their weekend.

The community is tight-knit, kind, and connected through love for surfskating, surfing, nature, the ocean, and each other. The slogan is "Meet your people." These are not passive consumers of a festival — they are the festival.

## Product Purpose

A mobile-first PWA that gives festival-goers the full program schedule and push notifications 15 minutes before each performance. No login, no friction — open, check, go back to having fun. Success means attendees never miss an act they wanted to see.

## Brand Personality

Retro-psychedelic surf/skate energy. Groovy, sun-drenched, unapologetically loud. The poster says it all: 1960s-70s concert poster meets Slovak outdoor festival. The personality in three words: **groovy, bold, sun-drenched**.

The brand is "Level Trevel Festival" running "Shred It Up" — a surf/skate community event where the visual identity is already fully formed in the print material. The app extends that identity to the screen, it does not reinvent it.

## Anti-references

- Generic music festival apps with dark backgrounds and neon accents (the Spotify/streaming aesthetic)
- Minimalist, clean, white-space-heavy "premium" festival apps
- shadcn/Tailwind default gray palette — zero brand identity
- Corporate or SaaS-adjacent designs: cards, data tables, neutral color systems
- 90s–2010s skate aesthetics (grunge, distressed type, dark/black-heavy) — this is retro *surf*, not skateboard street

## Design Principles

1. **Poster-first fidelity.** Every screen is an extension of the printed event identity. If a design choice would look out of place next to the poster, it does not belong in the app. The orange, the blue, the olive, the pink — they are fixed. Use them, don't interpret them.

2. **Legible in the wild.** Festival-goers use this outdoors in bright Slovak September sun, standing, distracted, possibly one-handed. Contrast cannot be sacrificed for style. Dark brown type on saturated backgrounds must pass WCAG AA.

3. **Committed, not cautious.** The poster is maximalist. The app must have the same confidence: no muted palettes, no hedged choices, no "let's dial it back a little." If it doesn't feel like the poster, push further.

4. **Community mirror.** The surfskate community has its own visual vocabulary — hand-crafted, sun-drenched, retro, irreverent. The app should feel like it was made by someone who surfs, not by a design agency hired to make a festival app.

5. **Delight through function.** The app solves a real problem — what's on, when, where. The delight is not decoration; it is wrapping that utility in the visual world of the event so that using the app feels like being at the festival.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum. The poster palette has inherent contrast challenges (warm colors on warm backgrounds); use the near-black dark brown (`#3A1A0A`) as the primary text color, never medium-gray. Support reduced motion. Slovak and English both must be fully legible — no font that handles diacritics poorly (ě, š, ž, č, ď, ť, ľ, ú, í, é, á).
